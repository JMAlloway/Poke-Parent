Grading Explained Calmly

TL;DR

  • Grading means sending a card to a professional company that examines its physical condition and seals it in a labeled case.
  • The big three companies are PSA, CGC, and BGS. Costs start around $15–30+ per card.
  • Turnaround time is typically weeks to months, depending on the service level.
  • Honest take: grading is usually unnecessary for kids' collections. It does not magically make a card worth more money.

What Is Grading?

Card grading is a service where you mail a card to a professional company. They examine it under magnification, assess its physical condition — corners, edges, surface, and centering — and assign it a numerical score, usually on a 1 to 10 scale. The card is then sealed inside a hard plastic case (called a "slab") with a label showing the grade.

A grade of 10 means the card is in essentially perfect condition. A grade of 7 means it has some visible wear. Most cards that have been handled by a child, even carefully, will not grade at a 10.

The Big Three Grading Companies

  • PSA (Professional Sports Authenticator) — The most widely recognized name in card grading. Their labels are the ones you see most often online and in shops.
  • CGC (Certified Guaranty Company) — Originally known for comic books, now well-established in card grading. Generally has faster turnaround times than PSA.
  • BGS (Beckett Grading Services) — Known for giving sub-grades in four categories (centering, corners, edges, surface), which some collectors appreciate for the detail.

All three are legitimate. The differences between them matter mainly to serious collectors. For a family exploring the idea of grading, any of the three is fine.

What It Costs

Grading is not free, and the costs add up quickly:

  • Base cost: Roughly $15–30 per card for standard service, depending on the company and current pricing.
  • Faster service: Expedited options can run $50–150+ per card.
  • Shipping both ways: You pay to mail the card to the company (with insurance and tracking) and they ship the slabbed card back. Budget another $10–20 for shipping.
  • Minimum submissions: Some companies require a minimum number of cards per order, or charge extra for single-card submissions.

For a single card, you are realistically looking at $25–50 all-in by the time you account for service fees and shipping.

How Long It Takes

Standard turnaround at most companies is measured in weeks, sometimes stretching to months during busy periods (holiday season, after a popular new set release). Expedited tiers are faster but cost significantly more. Do not expect to mail a card and get it back next week on a standard service tier.

The Honest Take: Is It Worth It?

For most kids' collections, grading is not necessary. Here is why:

  • Grading does not create value. A common card worth 50 cents does not become worth $50 because it received a grade of 10. Grading confirms condition — it does not change what the card is or how many exist.
  • Most cards are not worth the grading fee. If the grading cost is $25 and the card sells for $10 even in perfect condition, the math does not work out. Grading makes financial sense only for cards that are already sought-after and in excellent condition.
  • Kids' cards are usually handled. A card that has been shuffled in a deck, carried in a pocket, or traded on a playground will almost certainly not grade high enough for the grade to matter.
  • Slabbed cards cannot be played with. Once a card is sealed in a grading case, it stays there. For a child who wants to use, trade, or rearrange their cards, grading takes the card out of play permanently.

When Grading Might Make Sense

If your child pulls a genuinely rare card from a pack, handles it carefully from the moment it leaves the wrapper, and you can confirm the card has meaningful demand among collectors, grading can be worth considering. But even then, there is no rush. The card is not going anywhere. Take your time, do the research, and make a calm decision rather than a reactive one.

For the vast majority of families, a penny sleeve and a top loader provide all the protection a special card needs — at a fraction of the cost and with no wait time.
